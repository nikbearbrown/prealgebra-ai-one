# 1. Track the signed balance

*Below zero is a position. Adding or subtracting tells you how to move.*

Suppose a household ledger starts at −$7.50, representing a shortfall. A $48.00 deposit arrives. A $12.00 bill is then paid. What is available before shopping? Put zero on a number line. Start left of it at −7.50, move 48.00 to the right, then 12.00 to the left. The stops are −7.50, 40.50 and 28.50 dollars.

This book is for beginning and returning college learners. You can work with paper and a pencil. The aim is to explain why a step works and how you could catch it if it did not. All examples describe invented situations.

## Separate the sign from the action

A negative balance describes where you stand; a negative ledger entry describes a change. Adding a negative change decreases the balance. Subtracting a negative change reverses that change. For instance, reversing a mistaken −$12.00 charge is subtracting −$12.00, which adds $12.00. In the original three-step ledger you are paying a valid charge, so you add −12.00 rather than reverse it.

Test using the inverse: after the bill, 28.50 + 12.00 = 40.50. Removing the deposit from 40.50 recovers −7.50. Checking the ledger backwards is more informative than re-keying the same signs.

## Use grouping for what belongs together

Compare `48 − 2 × 12 = 24` with `(48 − 2) × 12 = 552`. The first expression describes subtracting two bills of $12 from $48. The second multiplies a difference by 12; it is not another convention for the same bill. Grouping lets a reader see which objects an operation applies to. Multiplication and division share priority and are evaluated left to right in a flat expression; addition and subtraction also share priority and are evaluated left to right. For instance, 12 ÷ 3 × 2 is 8, not 2.

If the ledger groups both changes first, the final balance remains −7.50 + (48.00 − 12.00) = 28.50. This works because subtracting 12 is adding −12 and these three additions can be regrouped. It does not give permission to regroup arbitrary subtractions: 20−(7−3) is 16, while (20−7)−3 is 10.

**Try it unaided.** Start at −$4, deposit $15, pay a $6 bill, then reverse that bill. Write each signed movement. Check the final result by temporarily removing the deposit.
