# 2. Three ways to write a part

*A fraction, a decimal and a percent can name the same amount without using the same unit.*

A recipe uses three quarters of a cup of sauce. If you imagine a cup split into four equal parts, the amount covers three. If you redraw the cup as 100 equal squares, mark 25 for each quarter and 75 in total. Now you are counting hundredths, not changing the amount.

`3/4 = (3×25)/(4×25) = 75/100 = 0.75 = 75%`.

The equivalents 3/4, 0.75 and 75% express a fraction of one cup in this context. To give an amount, keep the label: 3/4 **cup**. A percent by itself needs a reference whole. Seventy-five percent of two cups is 1.5 cups, not 0.75 cup.

## Check the conversion both ways

A decimal point puts digits into places. In 0.75, the 7 counts tenths and the 5 counts hundredths. Together they count 75 hundredths. Seventy-five out of a hundred equal parts is 75%. Going back, 75% means 75 per hundred; dividing numerator and denominator by 25 recovers 3/4. No faith in the decimal point is required.

Watch the zero: 0.075 is 75 thousandths, or 7.5 per hundred, not 75%. Use a quick order check. A quarter is 0.25; any claimed decimal much below 0.25 cannot represent three quarters.

Not every fraction has a terminating decimal. A third is exactly 1/3 and approximately 0.333 to three decimal places. Three rounded thirds of 0.333 make 0.999, not exactly 1. Keep fractions for exact intermediate work. A calculator's rounded display is a report about the value, not always the value itself.

A small reference table:

| Fraction of a whole | Decimal | Percent |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| 1/4 | 0.25 | 25% |
| 1/2 | 0.50 | 50% |
| 3/4 | 0.75 | 75% |
| 5/4 | 1.25 | 125% |

The last row means one whole and one quarter; percent can exceed 100 when the reference allows it.

**Try it unaided.** Write 7/20 as a decimal and percent by converting to hundredths. Then find 35% of $20. Explain why your two answers use different units.
