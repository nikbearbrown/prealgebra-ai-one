# A household budget in five checks

This fictional case is for pencil and paper. Show your work and label units. First attempt the main case and the separate transfer without AI, calculator feedback or an answer key.

1. The starting balance is −$7.50. Add a $48.00 deposit and pay a $12.00 bill. Record every balance and check one step backwards.
2. A separate recipe calls for 3/4 cup of sauce for three servings. Express the fraction in decimal and percent form, then find the amount for five servings. Check the cups per serving.
3. Buy at least 12 ounces of a dry staple. A whole small package is 6 oz for $1.50, a whole large one 10 oz for $2.00. Compare rates, find the cheapest feasible purchase, and explain why no overlooked basket wins.
4. If the household needs an $18.00 reserve after purchase and optional spending, give the equation at the boundary. Verify its solution, and say whether the entire next dollar could be spent.
5. A [bar chart](../figures/two-baselines.svg) compares balances after buying two small or two large. Using a $24.00 baseline, what do the bar lengths measure? State the actual dollar difference and show what a zero baseline would mean.

## Revision after the independent attempt

On paper, reverse one ledger step, recover one shelf price by multiplying the rate by ounces, and substitute the spending answer into the original equality. If permitted, you may instead ask for one hint on this fictional case: “Identify where my units or signs change and suggest one check, not the answer.” Check any hint by hand. Record the first result, the check, what you changed and your feedback source; if you used AI, name the tool and role. No paid tool or account is needed.

## Independent transfer

A different ledger starts at −$3.00, receives $27.00 and pays $9.00. A new recipe needs 2/3 cup for four servings; find six servings. Separately buy at least 9 oz of grain. Small packages are 5 oz for $1.20 and a large package is 9 oz for $2.10. Find the cheaper feasible basket, then compute the maximum optional expense with a $10.00 reserve. Substitute it back. Explain whether it is legitimate to infer that the large package will always cost less overall in a different shopping problem. Use no tool or key.
