# Prealgebra (AI Plus One)

## About the book

A household starts below zero, shops for a fixed amount of food, and tries to keep a reserve. This compact **rough draft** helps beginning or returning college learners explain and check the arithmetic behind that decision. It assumes familiarity with whole numbers, not equation-solving. By the end you can convert fractions, compare unit and total prices, solve a one-variable balance problem and explain what a chart actually shows. No account or calculator is required; expert and classroom review are pending.

## Chapter guide

**Part I: Build number sense**

1. [Track the signed balance](chapters/01-track-the-signed-balance.md). Use signs, number-line movements and operation order to check a ledger.
2. [Three ways to write a part](chapters/02-three-ways-to-write-a-part.md). Keep the reference whole in view when converting forms.

**Part II: Make and check a decision**

3. [Compare rates and baskets](chapters/03-compare-rates-and-baskets.md). Scale a recipe and find the least costly feasible whole-package purchase.
4. [Write the reserve equation](chapters/04-write-the-reserve-equation.md). Solve for spending at a reserve boundary and substitute back.
5. [Audit the chart baseline](chapters/05-audit-the-chart-baseline.md). Distinguish amounts above a threshold from total balances.

## How to teach or study it

Begin with the ledger movements and work toward a decision you can defend. Do the [household puzzle and transfer](practice/household-puzzle.md) without assistance, then check your reasoning with the [key](practice/answer-key.md). The [proposed teaching sequence](teaching/README.md) links the Blueprint and describes the hands-on and no-account routes. This is not a placement test or an established syllabus.
